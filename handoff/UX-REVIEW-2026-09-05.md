# StoneOS — UI/UX Review, 5 Sep 2026

Reviewer: UX pass against the deployed build at `https://p01--stoneos-web--fpd9p2zcyzpc.code.run`,
branch `pwa-mobile`, signed in as `sanjay` (owner). Source read alongside at
`packages/frontend/app/` and `packages/frontend/components/`.

**Method.** Every route was opened at a real Android phone profile (375×812, Pixel 8
user-agent, reloaded after the resize) and at desktop. Measurements below are actual
`getBoundingClientRect()` / computed-style / contrast readings taken in the live page, not
estimates. Console was read on every route: **zero client-side errors anywhere** — the app is
clean in that respect. Nothing was created, edited or deleted; no forms were submitted.

**Audience assumption applied throughout:** supervisors and operators on mid-range Android
phones, dusty or wet hands, bright daylight or dim shed light, in a hurry between machine
cycles, several more comfortable in Telugu than English. Owner and accountant on desktop.
Server in London, users in Andhra Pradesh — a measured `/auth/me` round trip from this
session was **433 ms**; from AP on 4G expect 500–800 ms, and every screen makes 1–6 of these.

## What already works well — do not break these

These are deliberate, correct decisions. Several are documented in comments in the source and
they should survive any redesign.

1. **Pinch-zoom is enabled** (`app/layout.tsx`, `maximumScale: 5`). Correct for reading a slab
   serial in bad light. Keep it. Nothing in `globals.css` fights it.
2. **16 px minimum font on inputs at ≤620 px** (`globals.css:254`). This is the threshold below
   which Android Chrome auto-zooms on focus and throws the layout sideways mid-entry. Someone
   knew that. Keep it.
3. **Tables scroll horizontally rather than reflowing into stacked cards** (`.table-scroll`,
   `globals.css:143`). Right call — a slab list is read by comparing columns. (The *reachability*
   of the far columns is a separate problem, below.)
4. **The operational-day default is computed, not typed.** `defaultOpDate()` in
   `app/dpr/page.tsx:32` and `app/polishing/page.tsx:36` rolls back a day before 7 am, matching
   the 7am–7am operational day. This silently saves the night-shift supervisor from mis-dating
   every log. Excellent.
5. **Serials are generated in bulk, not typed.** `submitCompletion` in `app/dpr/page.tsx` creates
   `V101/50/01…/47` server-side. The single biggest data-entry saving in the whole app.
6. **The nav renders only what the role can actually open.** `navLinksFor()` in
   `lib/routePolicy.ts` is shared by `AppNav`, `RouteAccessGuard` and mirrors the backend
   `RolesGuard`. A link can never point at a page that will 403.
7. **The role-denied state is written for a human** (`components/RouteAccessGuard.tsx`): it names
   the role and tells you who to ask, and distinguishes "your role lacks this" from "you have no
   role yet". Most apps ship a bare 403 here.
8. **Deliberately no offline write queue** (`public/sw.js` header comment). Given that a stale
   slab count causes real yard errors, refusing to cache API responses is the right trade. Do not
   let anyone "improve" this into offline-first without a conflict story.
9. **Contrast work already done and commented**: `--muted` was darkened to clear 4.5:1, and
   `--brass-dark` replaced `--brass` on buttons/active nav for the same reason. Nav links measure
   **9.71:1**, field labels **5.74:1**. Both comfortably pass.
10. **The one-time password panel** (`app/admin/users/page.tsx:146`) is honest — "shown once",
    `userSelect: all`, plus a Copy button. Correct handling of a credential you cannot re-read.
11. **`RouteAccessGuard` fails closed while the session resolves** rather than flashing a page the
    role may not see. Same for `useRole()` returning `undefined` mid-load.

## Cross-cutting — the app shell (`components/AppNav.tsx`, `app/globals.css`)

These cost time on *every* screen, so they rank above any single module's issues.

### X1. Numeric fields raise the alphabetic keyboard — 5 wasted taps per day-log, every day

**Observed.** Every numeric input in the app except `/setup/opening-inventory` is a bare
`<input className="field-input">`. Live measurement on `/dpr` and `/polishing` under the Pixel 8
UA: `type: "text"`, `inputMode: null` on *Expected Slabs, Runtime (hrs), Power Cut (min),
Downtime (min), Power (kWh), Slabs Today, Total Slabs Cut, Final Good Slabs, Length, Width,
Thickness*, and on `/sales` *Qty (sqft), Unit Price, GST, Loading, Transport, Invoiced Amt,
Actual Received*, and on `/expenses` *Amount*.

**Why it matters here.** Android opens the QWERTY keypad. The operator must hit `?123` to reach
digits, on every field, with dusty fingers, then hit `ABC` again if a text field follows. A
single cutting day-log has 5 numeric fields → **5 extra taps and 5 keyboard re-scans per log**.
Across two machines and ~300 operating days that is thousands of avoidable taps a year, and each
mode switch is a chance to enter `q` instead of `1`.

**Fix.** Keep `type="text"` (avoids the `type=number` scroll-wheel and spinner problems on
desktop) and add `inputMode`:
- whole counts — `inputMode="numeric"` on Expected Slabs, Power Cut (min), Downtime (min),
  Slabs Today, Total Slabs Cut, Final Good Slabs (`app/dpr/page.tsx`), Downtime (min)
  (`app/polishing/page.tsx`);
- decimals — `inputMode="decimal"` on Runtime (hrs), Power (kWh), Length/Width/Thickness
  (`app/dpr/page.tsx`), Runtime/Power (`app/polishing/page.tsx`), Qty/Unit Price/GST/Loading/
  Transport/Invoiced/Received (`app/sales/page.tsx`), Amount (`app/expenses/page.tsx`).

Cheapest implementation: add a `numeric` / `decimal` variant to the shared `.field-input` call
sites, or introduce a small `<NumField>` wrapper next to `components/Ticket.tsx` so this can
never drift again. **This is the single highest-value change in the review.**

### X2. The header eats 33–60 % of the phone screen before any work appears

**Observed** (375×812, measured `.stamp` height):

| Route | `.stamp` height | % of 812 px viewport |
|---|---|---|
| `/dashboard`, `/expenses` | 268 px | 33 % |
| `/setup/opening-inventory`, `/copilot` | 273 px | 34 % |
| `/dpr` | 286 px | 35 % |
| **`/polishing`** | **486 px** | **60 %** |

Nine nav chips wrap to three rows at 44 px each, plus title and sub-line. On `/polishing` it is
worse (see P1). The operator opens the app between machine cycles and sees navigation, not work.

**Why it matters.** Every entry session begins with a scroll past a menu the operator does not
need — they came to log one thing. It also pushes the "Save" button so far down that the error
message rendered *below* it is off-screen (see D2).

**Fix (in order of effort).**
- Cheapest: collapse the nav to a single row of the 3–4 routes the signed-in role uses most plus
  a "More" chip, at `@media (max-width: 620px)` in `globals.css` around `.nav-links`.
- Better: move navigation to a fixed bottom bar on phones — thumb-reachable, and it frees the
  entire top of the screen. `.app-shell` already reserves `padding-bottom: 84px` (96 px in
  standalone) for exactly this shape.
- Either way, make `.stamp-sub` collapse on phones; `STONEOS · VEDAM GRANITES · OPERATIONAL DAY
  = 7AM–7AM` wraps to two lines and tells a daily user nothing.

### X3. "Sign out" is invisible — 1.05:1 contrast — on a shared factory phone

**Observed.** `.user-button-signout` (`globals.css:316`) sets `color: var(--ink)` (#241F1A) but
it sits inside `.stamp`, whose background is `--graphite` (#1C1B1A). Measured contrast:
**1.05:1**. The username next to it measures **2.87:1** (`--muted` on graphite). Confirmed
visually — in the screenshots the button reads as an empty outlined box.

**Why it matters.** These are shared floor phones. The comment in `components/UserButton.tsx`
says sign-out exists precisely so "the next person does not inherit the session" — and it is the
one control nobody can see. In bright daylight on a phone at 40 % brightness it is simply gone.

**Fix.** In `globals.css`, change `.user-button-signout` to `color: var(--stone-100);
border-color: #3a3733;` and `.user-button-name` to `color: var(--stone-300)` (which already
measures 9.71:1 on graphite for `.nav-links a`). Also bump the button to `min-height: 44px;
padding: 10px 14px` under the `@media (max-width: 620px)` block — it currently measures
**24 px × 67 px**, well under the 44 px Android target the rest of the sheet respects.

### X4. A failed save shows the raw API string

**Observed.** `lib/api.ts:27` throws ``new Error(`API error ${res.status}: ${body}`)`` and every
page renders `e.message` verbatim. A supervisor gets
`API error 500: {"statusCode":500,"message":"Internal server error"}` in 12.5 px rust text.
`/expenses` is the only page that translates anything (`app/expenses/page.tsx:91` maps 400 →
"Check the category/vehicle combination").

**Why it matters.** For a Telugu-first operator this is noise; they cannot tell "the network
dropped, try again" from "you entered something impossible" from "you are not allowed to do
this". Both lead to the same wrong action: tap Save again.

**Fix.** Add a `describeApiError(e)` helper next to `lib/api.ts` mapping status → a short
sentence with a next action:
- `TypeError`/`Failed to fetch` → "No connection. Your entry is still here — reconnect and press
  Save again."
- `401` → "Signed out. Sign in and re-enter."
- `403` → "Your role cannot do this. Ask an owner or manager."
- `400`/`422` → surface the backend `message` field only, not the JSON envelope.
- `5xx` → "The server had a problem. Nothing was saved. Try again in a moment."
Call it from every `catch` in `app/dpr`, `app/polishing`, `app/sales`, `app/expenses`,
`app/admin/users`, `app/setup/opening-inventory`. Keep the raw string behind a "Details" toggle
for the owner.

### X5. No offline indicator, and the app never acknowledges the London round trip

**Observed.** `navigator.onLine` is not consulted anywhere. The service worker
(`public/sw.js`) is registered and live (`stoneos-shell-v1` cache present, scope `/`), navigation
is network-first with an `offline.html` fallback — all correct — but that only fires on a
*page navigation*. If the signal drops **mid-entry**, which is the real scenario behind thick
shed walls, the operator taps Save, `fetch` rejects with `TypeError: Failed to fetch`, and that
string appears in small rust text. There is no "you are offline" banner and no retry affordance.

Also: `offline.html` promises *"Nothing you entered has been lost"*. That is true only because
the SPA state survives — but the moment the operator reloads or navigates, it is lost, and the
page they are reading is exactly the one shown *after* a navigation failed.

**Fix.**
- Add a small persistent offline strip to `app/layout.tsx` (a client component beside
  `ServiceWorker.tsx`) listening to `online`/`offline` events: a `--rust` bar reading "Offline —
  nothing will save until the signal returns."
- On a failed write, keep the form populated (it already is) and swap the Save button to
  "Retry save" rather than showing a paragraph of text.
- Soften the `offline.html` copy to "Anything you had open on screen is still there — go back and
  press Save again," which is the honest claim.

### X6. Cold start shows a bare "Loading…" for half a second on every launch

**Observed.** `components/AuthGate.tsx:20` blocks the entire app behind a centred `Loading…`
while `/auth/me` resolves — measured at **433 ms** from this session, realistically 600–900 ms
from Andhra Pradesh, longer on a container cold start. `manifest.ts` sets
`background_color: #EDEAE4` so the splash does not flash white (good), and then the app shows an
unstyled grey word on a blank page.

**Fix.** Replace the `AuthGate` fallback with the app's own `.stamp` header plus a skeleton
ticket — the same shape `LoadingDashboard` in `app/dashboard/page.tsx:35` already renders. The
user sees the app, not a hang. Zero backend work.

### X7. No form-level "in flight" feedback on the most-used button

**Observed.** `globals.css` defines `.primary-btn:disabled { opacity: .6 }` and
`.ghost-btn:disabled` — but there is **no `.mini-btn:disabled` rule** (verified by enumerating
the stylesheet). `/dpr`'s Save Day Log is a `.mini-btn` whose label never changes to "Saving…".
So during a 400–800 ms round trip to London, tapping Save produces **no visible change at all**.

**Why it matters.** The operator assumes the tap missed and taps again. On `/dpr` a double tap
posts a second day-log.

**Fix.** Add `.mini-btn:disabled { opacity: .6; cursor: default; }` to `globals.css` and change
the `/dpr` button label to `{status === "saving" ? "Saving…" : "Save Day Log"}`, matching what
`/polishing`, `/sales` and `/expenses` already do.

## `/dpr` — Production (highest traffic)

### D1. The daily action is styled as the secondary button; the rare action gets the primary

**Observed.** "Start Cutting" (`app/dpr/page.tsx:229`) — done maybe twice a week — is a
full-width brass `.primary-btn`. "Save Day Log" (`app/dpr/page.tsx:285`) — done every single day
per active session, the whole reason the page exists — is a small right-aligned `.mini-btn`.

**Why it matters.** Visual weight is the fastest instruction you can give a hurried user. Right
now the loudest thing on the page is the button they should almost never press, and it sits
*above* the one they always press.

**Fix.** In `app/dpr/page.tsx`, swap the classes: make Save Day Log
`className="primary-btn"` (it then picks up the mobile `width: 100%; min-height: 52px` rule) and
demote Start Cutting to `.mini-btn`. Consider collapsing the whole "Allocate Block to B-21"
ticket behind a disclosure when at least one session is active, so the daily screen opens
directly on the day-log form.

### D2. The error message renders at the bottom of the page, hundreds of pixels below the button

**Observed.** `errorMsg` is rendered once, at `app/dpr/page.tsx:411`, *after* every session
ticket and the completed-results tickets — the last element in the component. With one active
session the phone page is ~1,900 px tall; the operator taps Save Day Log at roughly 1,200 px and
the failure message appears at ~1,850 px, off-screen. With two active sessions it is worse.

**Why it matters.** The operator taps Save, sees nothing (see X7), scrolls away, and believes the
day was logged. The day is not logged. This is a silent data-loss path on the highest-traffic
screen.

**Fix.** Move the error into the session's own `Ticket`, immediately above the Save button —
`app/dpr/page.tsx` around line 284 — and scope it per session id (see D3). Same change for the
allocate ticket.

### D3. One global `status` and `errorMsg` for all sessions

**Observed.** `status` and `errorMsg` (`app/dpr/page.tsx:25–26`) are single values shared by the
allocate form and every active session. Saving session A disables session B's Save button and
paints its icon green; an error from session A appears as if it belonged to B.

**Fix.** Key both by session id, e.g. `const [status, setStatus] = useState<Record<string,
Status>>({})` with a separate `allocStatus`, mirroring how `dayLogs` and `completionForm` are
already keyed. Low risk, contained to `app/dpr/page.tsx`.

### D4. "Complete" has no confirmation and is irreversible

**Observed.** `Complete` → fill 2 required fields → `Confirm Completion` posts
`/cutting-sessions/{id}/complete`, which bulk-generates every slab serial and closes the block.
There is no summary-and-confirm step and no undo. The only guard is the required-field check at
`app/dpr/page.tsx:157`.

The preview text at line 318 is good — it computes damaged count and shows the serial range —
but it is 12 px `--muted` body copy, easy to skim past on a phone, and it renders *above* the
optional per-slab override block, so on a 47-slab batch with overrides open it can be 3,000 px
away from the Confirm button.

**Why it matters.** A typo of 470 instead of 47 in "Final Good Slabs" mints 470 inventory rows
against a block. Recovering from that is an owner's evening.

**Fix.** (a) Promote the preview into a `.stat-row` of three `.stat-card`s (Cut / Good / Damaged)
directly above the Confirm button, so the numbers are 20 px mono and unmissable. (b) Add a plain
sanity check before submit in `submitCompletion`: refuse when `finalGoodSlabCount >
totalSlabsCut`, and warn when `totalSlabsCut` differs from `expectedSlabCount` by more than ~30 %.
(c) Add a `window.confirm` naming the block and the count, as `app/admin/users/page.tsx:93`
already does for revoke.

### D5. Per-slab overrides render N full cards inside a 320 px scroll box

**Observed.** Ticking "different sizes for some slabs?" (`app/dpr/page.tsx:338`) renders one
`.row-card` per good slab inside `maxHeight: 320, overflowY: auto`. At 47 slabs on a phone —
where `.row-grid` collapses to one column, so each card is 3 stacked 48 px inputs plus label —
that is ~47 × 250 px ≈ **11,700 px of content inside a 320 px window**, nested inside the page's
own scroll.

**Why it matters.** Nested scrolling on Android is a known thumb trap: a swipe that starts one
pixel outside the box scrolls the page instead, and the user loses their place. Editing slab #38
means ~35 blind flicks.

**Fix.** This is a rare mixed-size batch. Replace the full list with an "add an exception" flow:
a single row of *Slab # / Length / Width / Thickness* plus "+ Add exception", listing only the
sequences that actually differ — which is exactly what `buildSlabOverrides()` already sends. Same
file; it also makes the payload obvious to the user.

### D6. The block dropdown gives no reason when it is empty

**Observed.** With no `in_stock` blocks, the "Raw Block (in stock)" select shows only "Select…".
There is no hint that blocks exist but are all under cutting, or that none have been added.
(In this QA dataset there were 2 in stock and 2 elsewhere.)

**Fix.** In `app/dpr/page.tsx`, when `inStockBlocks.length === 0` render an `.empty-note` in place
of the select: "No blocks in stock. All 4 blocks are on machines — complete a session first, or
add stock in Opening Count." Colour-independent, actionable, names the next step.

### D7. Taps for the daily job (measured on a phone)

Logging one cutting day, cold from the home screen: **~15 taps + 5 keyboard mode switches +
2 scroll passes**. Of those, 5 taps are pure keyboard switching (X1), 1–2 are scrolling past the
allocate ticket (D1), and the operational date is free thanks to `defaultOpDate()`. Fixing X1 and
D1 alone takes this to ~9 taps with no mode switches.

Completing a session adds 1 tap (Complete) + 6 fields (4 numeric → 4 more keyboard switches
today) + 1 Confirm.

## `/sales`

### S1. One line item is ~1,400 px of vertical scroll on a phone

**Observed.** `.row-grid` collapses to a single column at ≤620 px (`globals.css:249`), so each
line item becomes 8–10 stacked 48 px inputs with labels. A one-line order already scrolls past
the fold twice; a four-line order is roughly 5,600 px of scroll and ~60 taps (6 numeric fields ×
tap + `?123`, plus variety and the payment select).

**Why it matters.** Sales orders are entered by the owner/accountant on desktop most of the time,
which is why this ranks below `/dpr` — but the moment a supervisor enters one at the gate on a
phone it is punishing, and the running Row Total is 10 fields away from the inputs that drive it.

**Fix.** Two columns for the *numeric* pairs on phones. In `globals.css`, inside the ≤620 px
block, add `.row-grid { grid-template-columns: 1fr 1fr; }` and let long fields opt out with a
span class (`.field--wide { grid-column: 1 / -1 }`) applied to Variety and Row Total in
`app/sales/page.tsx`. Qty/Unit Price and Loading/Transport are natural pairs and halve the
scroll. Also move the Row Total readout to the top of each `.row-card`, where it is visible while
you type.

### S2. On a phone the Recent Orders table hides the Total

**Observed.** `.table-scroll` on `/sales` scrolls horizontally as designed; on `/expenses` the
overflow measures **293 px** and on `/admin/users` **391 px** — the rightmost columns (Amount,
Actions) are entirely off-screen. Screenshot of `/sales` confirms the Total and Payment badges
sit past the right edge; the customer names are additionally prefixed with the QA marker so they
truncate.

**Why it matters.** Horizontal scroll is the right structural choice, but there is no signal that
more columns exist beyond a thin scrollbar, and the column people look for (money) is the one
placed last.

**Fix.** In `globals.css`, (a) add a fade/shadow affordance on `.table-scroll` so it reads as
scrollable — e.g. `background: linear-gradient(...)` with `background-attachment: local, scroll`,
the standard scroll-shadow trick; and (b) pin the first column on phones
(`.list-table td:first-child, .list-table th:first-child { position: sticky; left: 0; background:
#FBFAF7; }`) so the serial/date stays visible while you scroll to the number. In
`app/sales/page.tsx` and `app/expenses/page.tsx`, reorder the columns so the amount sits second,
right after the date.

### S3. `Row Total` looks like an editable field

**Observed.** `app/sales/page.tsx:210` renders the computed total as a
`<div className="field-input mono">` with a slightly different background. It has an input's
border, radius, padding and 48 px height on mobile.

**Fix.** Give it its own class in `globals.css` — `.field-readout { border: none; background:
transparent; font-size: 18px; font-weight: 700; padding: 0; }` — so the money reads as an output,
and drop the fake border. Same treatment would suit the `.totals-strip .value` (currently 16 px
in a 15-px-dense page; the order total should be the biggest number on screen).

### S4. Customer is required but nothing marks the moment it is missing

**Observed.** The label carries `<span className="required-mark">*</span>` (good), but the check
only runs on submit (`app/sales/page.tsx:82`), and the resulting message renders near the top of
the ticket while Save Order is at the very bottom — off-screen on a phone.

**Fix.** Same pattern as D2: render `errorMsg` immediately above the Save button, and
additionally set `aria-invalid` / a `--rust` border on the customer select so the failure is
carried by more than a sentence the user has scrolled past.

## `/polishing`

### P1. The header is 486 px tall — 60 % of the phone screen — because of a nested flex row

**Observed.** `app/polishing/page.tsx:127` wraps the date box and `<AppNav />` in
`<div style={{ display: "flex", alignItems: "center", gap: 12 }}>`. The `@media (max-width:620px)`
rule only stacks `.stamp` itself; this inner row is untouched, so the nav chips are forced into a
narrow right-hand column and wrap into **seven** rows. Measured `.stamp` height: **486 px** versus
268 px on `/dashboard`. On a 812 px screen, the first form field is below the fold.

**Fix.** Delete the inline `style` on that wrapper and give it a class, e.g. `.stamp-actions`, then
in `globals.css`'s ≤620 px block: `.stamp-actions { display: flex; flex-direction: column;
align-items: stretch; gap: 10px; width: 100%; }`. That alone recovers ~200 px on the busiest
polishing screen.

### P2. The operational-date control is a 19 px tap target

**Observed.** The date input inside `.date-box` measures **19 px × 121 px**. `.date-box input`
(`globals.css:172`) deliberately strips the border and never receives the `.field-input` mobile
treatment (16 px font, 48 px min-height). It is also dark-on-dark (`--stone-100` on
`--graphite-soft`), which is fine for contrast but tiny.

**Why it matters.** This control decides which day's runs are shown *and* which day a new run is
recorded against. Mis-tapping it silently files a run under yesterday. 19 px with wet fingers is
a coin flip.

**Fix.** In `globals.css`, inside the ≤620 px block add `.date-box { padding: 10px 12px; }
.date-box input { font-size: 16px; min-height: 44px; width: 100%; }`.

### P3. Picking slabs: 54 checkboxes, 13 px each, in a 260 px nested scroller, no search

**Observed.** Measured live: **54 eligible slabs**, checkbox size **13 × 13 px**, list container
**260 px tall** with `overflowY: auto` (`app/polishing/page.tsx:190`), inside the page's own
scroll. At one card per row on a phone, about 5 of 54 are visible at a time. There is no search,
no filter by block or variety, no "select all", and no grouping.

**Why it matters.** LPM runs are batches — the operator loads a liner's worth of slabs, which in
practice are consecutive serials from the same block. Today, selecting 20 consecutive slabs means
~20 flicks inside a nested scroller and 20 taps on 13 px boxes, with no way to confirm you got the
right range. This is the second-worst time sink in the app after X1.

**Fix, in `app/polishing/page.tsx`:**
- The `<label className="row-card">` is already 44 px tall and the whole card toggles — good. But
  the 13 px `<input type="checkbox">` should get `width: 22px; height: 22px; accent-color:
  var(--brass-dark);` (add a `.slab-check` class in `globals.css`) so the checked state is legible
  at arm's length in bright light.
- Add a serial filter input above the list (`inputMode="numeric"` won't do — serials are
  alphanumeric) that substring-matches `s.slabSerial`.
- Group `eligibleSlabs` by the block serial prefix (everything before the first `/`) with a
  per-group "Select all N" — this matches how slabs physically arrive at the LPM.
- Show the selected serials as removable chips under the list so the operator can verify the batch
  without scrolling back through 54 rows. The count alone ("12 selected") does not let them check.
- Raise `maxHeight` to something like `50vh` on phones, or drop the nested scroller entirely and
  let the page scroll — the nested box is the part that fights the thumb.

### P4. "Grinding" vs "Polishing" is a consequential choice hidden in a plain select

**Observed.** `stage` defaults to `"polishing"` (`app/polishing/page.tsx:44`). Per the file's own
comment, a *Polishing* run advances the slab to `salesStatus: 'polished'` and a *Grinding* run
does not. The two are visually identical `select.field-input`s, and nothing on screen says the
outcome differs.

**Why it matters.** Choosing wrong either makes unfinished slabs sellable or leaves finished ones
invisible to `/sales`. The operator has no way to know that from the UI.

**Fix.** Replace the select with two large segmented buttons (reuse the `.mini-btn` active
styling from `app/setup/opening-inventory/page.tsx:104` — that pattern already exists) and put a
one-line consequence under the selection: "Polishing marks these slabs ready to sell." /
"Grinding is recorded only — the slabs stay unfinished."

### P5. The submit button says "Record Polishing Run" but the errors sit above the slab list

Same class of problem as D2/S4: `errorMsg` renders at `app/polishing/page.tsx:209`, above the
button — acceptable here — but the *load* error and empty state are inside the slab picker while
the failure message is outside it. Keep one convention across the app: error immediately above
the button that triggered it.

## `/dashboard`

### DB1. Every headline number reads ₹0 and nothing explains why

**Observed.** With 2 sales orders and 5 expenses in the database, the dashboard shows
`Sqft Sold 0`, `Invoiced ₹0`, `Actually Received ₹0`, `Total Spend ₹0` — while the Recent Activity
table below lists those very orders. Cause: `isoDaysAgo(30)`/`isoToday()`
(`app/dashboard/page.tsx:12–17`) hard-code a trailing-30-day window, and the seeded records fall
outside it.

**Why it matters.** The owner cannot tell "there were no sales in the last 30 days" from "the
query is broken" from "the API failed". A stat card showing ₹0 with no context is the least
trustworthy element you can put in front of the person who signs the cheques — especially when
the table underneath contradicts it.

**Fix, in `app/dashboard/page.tsx`:**
- Print the actual window under the ticket subtitle: "1 Aug – 5 Sep 2026" instead of the abstract
  "Trailing 30 Days".
- When every figure in a card is zero, replace the `.stat-row` with an `.empty-note`: "No sales
  recorded in this window." That distinguishes empty from broken.
- Add a date-range control (This month / Last 30 days / Custom). The `from`/`to` params are
  already plumbed to `/daily-sales-summary` and `/expenses`, so this is front-end only.

### DB2. Six API calls fire on every visit and one failure blanks the whole page

**Observed.** `load()` (`app/dashboard/page.tsx:87`) runs six requests in one `Promise.all`; any
rejection lands in the shared `catch` and the page renders a single "Couldn't load dashboard data:
<raw message>" instead of the five sections. At ~450 ms each to London this is also the app's
slowest screen, and it is `start_url` in `manifest.ts` — the first thing anyone sees.

**Fix.** Use `Promise.allSettled` and render each `Ticket` independently, with a per-card error
row. The dashboard is five unrelated summaries; there is no reason a failing `/vehicles` should
hide the block stock. (The error path itself is a real improvement over the previous permanent
"Loading…" — the comment at line 133 says so — this is the next step.)

### DB3. Skeletons, not a word

`Loading…` in a bare ticket for ~1 s. Replace with five `.stat-card`-shaped skeletons; the
`dashboard-fade-in` animation already exists to make the swap feel intentional.

## `/setup/opening-inventory`

### O1. A raw JavaScript exception is displayed to the user, on the first screen a new factory sees

**Observed, live, on this deployment:**

> `Failed to execute 'json' on 'Response': Unexpected end of JSON input`

rendered in a rust-bordered ticket above "Start the opening count". Cause: `GET
/opening-inventory` returns an empty body when no count exists, and `apiFetch`
(`lib/api.ts:29`) unconditionally calls `res.json()`. `load()` catches the resulting `TypeError`
and shows `e.message`.

**Why it matters.** This is not an edge case — it is the *normal* state of a factory that has not
yet counted, i.e. every first-time user. The single most important onboarding screen in the
product opens with a browser error message. It also makes the page look broken when it is fine.

**Fix.** In `lib/api.ts`, guard the parse:
```ts
if (res.status === 204) return null;
const text = await res.text();
return text ? JSON.parse(text) : null;
```
That fixes it globally. `app/setup/opening-inventory/page.tsx:48` already handles a null snapshot
correctly (`step` stays 0, `StartStep` renders), so no page change is needed.

### O2. The step strip is built from `.nav-links` and `.mini-btn`, not a progress component

**Observed.** `app/setup/opening-inventory/page.tsx:96` reuses `className="nav-links"` for the
wizard steps and hand-rolls the active/done colours inline. Visually the steps are
indistinguishable from the app's navigation chips sitting 40 px above them, and "done" is carried
by a pale green fill alone — **colour is the only signal**.

**Fix.** Give it its own `.step-strip` in `globals.css`, and add a `<Check size={12} />` to
completed steps and `aria-current="step"` to the active one so state survives colour-blindness,
glare and a greyscale screenshot.

### O3. This module quietly uses a *different* input convention from the rest of the app

**Observed.** `/setup/opening-inventory` is the only page using `type="number"`
(lines 237, 241, 293, 297, 301) and the only one using `<label htmlFor>` with sibling inputs
rather than the `<label className="field">` wrapper used everywhere else. It also defines its own
local `Ticket` component (line 172) instead of importing `components/Ticket.tsx`, and that local
copy renders the title as a `<div>` rather than an `<h2>`.

**Why it matters.** Two consequences beyond tidiness: the local `Ticket` drops the heading
semantics (screen-reader and keyboard heading navigation on desktop), and `type="number"` on
desktop lets a scroll wheel silently change an opening *value* — the number that seeds the
factory's books.

**Fix.** Delete the local `Ticket` and import the shared one. Move the numeric inputs to
`type="text" inputMode="decimal"` to match the rest of the app (this also gains the phone
keypad without the wheel hazard). This module having its own conventions is the clearest case of
design-system drift in the codebase.

### O4. Adding 200 slabs one at a time

**Observed.** `SlabStep` adds one slab per round trip to London (`onAdd` → POST → `load()` refetch
of the whole snapshot). A real opening count is hundreds of rows. At ~450 ms per POST plus a full
snapshot refetch, this is minutes of thumb-waiting with no bulk path and no paste/CSV import.

**Fix.** Out of scope for a CSS pass, but flag it: add a "serial range" shortcut
(`V101/50/01`–`V101/50/47`, one variety, one size) that posts once, and stop refetching the entire
snapshot after every add — append the created line to local state instead. The endpoint already
returns the created row.

## `/admin/users`

### A1. Revoke and Reset are 24 px tall and live in an off-screen column

**Observed.** `.ghost-btn` measures **78 × 24 px** ("Revoke") and **70 × 24 px** ("Reset"). The

**Why it matters.** These are the two most destructive actions in the product (immediate sign-out;

from the `ROLES` array (line 11) with no description of what each can do. The owner provisioning a

**Fix.** Add a one-line hint under the Role select, driven by the selected value, e.g. "Supervisor
— can log production, polishing, sales and expenses." The role→route mapping is already declared
in `lib/routePolicy.ts` and can generate this text.

## `/copilot`

### C1. The question box is 22 pixels wide on a phone — the feature is unusable

**Observed, measured live at 375×812:** the input is **22 px wide**; the "Ask" button is **291 px**.
Cause: `app/copilot/page.tsx:169` uses `<div style={{ display: "flex", gap: 8 }}>` with

**Why it matters.** Copilot is owner-only, so it is not a floor-worker blocker — but the owner

**Fix.** Give the row a class (`.ask-row`) and in `globals.css`'s ≤620 px block:

state (`.field-input` has no `:disabled` rule in `globals.css`).

**Fix.** Add `.field-input:disabled { background: var(--stone-200); color: var(--muted); }` and an

### C3. The empty state is the best in the app — keep it

`app/copilot/page.tsx:109` shows two concrete example questions rather than "No results". This is

## Accessibility (desktop, owner and accountant)

Findings not already covered above.

- **Labels are correctly tied to inputs everywhere.** Most forms use the wrapping
  `<label className="field">` pattern; `/setup/opening-inventory` and `/sign-in` use
  `htmlFor`/`id`. Both are valid. `app/polishing/page.tsx:131` has an explicit `aria-label` on the
  bare date input. This is better than most in-house apps.
- **Focus is visible on nav links only.** `globals.css:58` defines `.nav-links a:focus-visible`;
  there is **no focus style on `.primary-btn`, `.mini-btn`, `.ghost-btn`, `.add-btn` or
  `.row-remove`**. A keyboard user tabbing the accountant's expense form cannot see where they
  are. **Fix:** add one shared rule — `.primary-btn:focus-visible, .mini-btn:focus-visible,
  .ghost-btn:focus-visible, .add-btn:focus-visible, .row-remove:focus-visible { outline: 2px solid
  var(--brass-dark); outline-offset: 2px; }`.
- **Heading structure is sound** — one `<h1 className="stamp-title">` per page, `<h2>` per ticket
  via `components/Ticket.tsx` — **except** the local `Ticket` in
  `app/setup/opening-inventory/page.tsx:179`, which emits a `<div>`, and `/sign-in`, which has no
  `<h1>` at all (the title is a `<div className="ticket-title">`).
- **No `<form>` element on any data-entry page except `/sign-in`.** Every other page uses a bare
  button with `onClick`. Consequence: pressing Enter in a field never submits, so a desktop
  accountant entering expenses must reach for the mouse on every record. **Fix:** wrap each entry
  block in `<form onSubmit={...}>` with the action button as `type="submit"` — biggest desktop
  time saving available, and it costs almost nothing.
- **Colour as sole carrier of meaning** appears in three places: the wizard "done" step
  (`/setup/opening-inventory`, O2), the revoked-user opacity (`/admin/users`, A2), and the
  `belowBenchmark` badge (`/recovery-ratio`, R2). Each is fixed by adding an icon or a word.
- **Live regions**: none. Status changes ("Saved", error text) are inserted into the DOM with no
  `role="status"` / `aria-live="polite"`, so a screen-reader user gets no confirmation a save
  worked. Add `role="status"` to the error/`saved` containers.
- **`window.confirm`** in `app/admin/users/page.tsx:94` is accessible and unmissable — keep it, and
  extend the same pattern to `/dpr`'s Confirm Completion (D4).

## PWA behaviour — what actually happens when the signal drops

Verified live: one service worker registered at scope `/`, cache `stoneos-shell-v1` present,
`public/sw.js` active.

| Moment connectivity drops | What happens today | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Before opening the app | `offline.html` renders — branded, honest, has a Try again button | Good |
| While on a page, then user navigates | `offline.html` — but any typed-and-unsaved form data is destroyed | Its own copy claims otherwise (X5) |
| Mid-entry, user taps Save | `TypeError: Failed to fetch` in 12.5 px rust text at the bottom of the page. No offline banner, no retry button, no queue | **The weak point** |
| Signal returns | Nothing happens automatically; the user must find the error, scroll back, and tap Save again | Needs the retry affordance from X4/X5 |

The *architecture* is right — refusing to cache API responses (`public/sw.js:44–45`) is the correct
call for an app that is the source of truth for what is standing in the yard, and the reasoning is
documented. The gap is entirely in the UI layer: the app never tells the user it is offline, and a
failed write is indistinguishable from a server error. Fix X5 and X4 and the PWA story is
complete without touching the service worker.

One smaller note: `sw.js`'s fetch handler falls through without calling `respondWith` for any
same-origin GET that is neither a navigation nor `/_next/static` nor `/icons` — e.g.
`/manifest.webmanifest`, `/offline.html` fetched directly. Harmless (the browser handles it), but
the `if` at line 56 has no `else`, which reads like an oversight; add a comment or an explicit
`return`.

## Design-system drift

`app/globals.css` is a genuinely good sheet — 344 lines, tokenised, with the reasoning for the
awkward decisions written down. The drift is concentrated in three places:

1. **`app/setup/opening-inventory/page.tsx`** — its own local `Ticket`, its own label pattern, its
   own input type, `.nav-links` reused as a progress strip, and inline colour literals
   (`#DCE6D3` at line 107, which is `.badge.invoiced`'s background). See O2/O3.
2. **Inline styles carrying design decisions.** `style={{ color: "#6B6255" }}` appears as a literal
   in `app/dpr/page.tsx:315`, `:319`, `:405` and `app/copilot/page.tsx:131` instead of
   `var(--muted)`; `#555` appears in `app/dpr/page.tsx:396` and `app/polishing/page.tsx:236` and
   is in no token; `var(--paper-shade, #F1EDE4)` in `app/admin/users/page.tsx:157` references a
   token that **does not exist** in `globals.css` and silently falls back. Sweep these into tokens.
3. **The "error message" pattern is invented per page** — five different combinations of colour,
   size, margin and placement across `/dpr`, `/polishing`, `/sales`, `/expenses`,
   `/setup/opening-inventory`. Extract one `<FormError>` component beside `components/Ticket.tsx`
   and use it everywhere. This is what makes X4/D2/S4 a one-file fix rather than six.

Also worth noting: the button hierarchy is inverted on the busiest page (D1) and the "saving"
label convention differs (`/polishing`, `/sales`, `/expenses` say "Saving…"; `/dpr` says nothing;
`/admin/users` says "Issuing…"). Pick one.
